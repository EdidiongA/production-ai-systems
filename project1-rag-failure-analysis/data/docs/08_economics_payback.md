# System Economics and Payback Analysis

Capital cost breakdown for a representative 5.5 kVA / 5.6 kWp / 18 kWh LiFePO4 system in Nigeria (2026 prices): panels 25-30 percent, battery bank 40-45 percent, inverter 15-18 percent, balance-of-system (mounting, cabling, protection, installation) 12-15 percent.

Payback is driven by displaced generator fuel and grid tariff. Band A grid tariffs reached N225/kWh in 2024-2025; petrol self-generation costs N350-500/kWh. A system displacing 15 kWh/day of mixed generator and Band A consumption saves roughly N1.5-2.2 million per year, giving a 2.5-4 year simple payback on a N6-8 million installation.

Battery replacement is the dominant lifecycle cost. At 4,000 cycles and one cycle per day, an 18 kWh LiFePO4 bank lasts 10+ years; budget its replacement at 40 percent of original system cost in year 10-12 (real prices have fallen about 10 percent annually).

Sizing economics: oversizing PV is cheap (panels are the cheapest component per kWh delivered) while oversizing the battery is expensive. The efficient design points PV at 1.3-1.6x the inverter rating and the battery at 1.2-1.5x average nightly consumption.

Financing note: naira devaluation makes imported components track USD; buyers who can stage purchases should buy the battery last, since LiFePO4 USD prices continue to decline while panel prices have flattened.
