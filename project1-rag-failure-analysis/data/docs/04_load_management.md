# Load Management and the Inverter AC Upgrade

Air conditioning dominates residential energy budgets in tropical climates. A conventional fixed-speed 1.5 HP (about 1.1 kW) split AC draws 1,100-1,300 W while the compressor runs and surges 3-4x at compressor start. Duty-cycling means it consumes roughly 6-9 kWh over an 8-hour night.

An inverter-drive AC of the same cooling capacity modulates compressor speed instead of cycling. Real-world consumption for a 1.5 HP inverter AC over the same 8-hour night is 3-4.5 kWh, a 40-55 percent reduction, and its soft ramp eliminates the start surge that trips overload faults on 5 kVA-class hybrid inverters.

For a solar-plus-storage home, replacing a fixed-speed AC with an inverter AC is usually the highest-leverage single upgrade: it simultaneously reduces battery drain overnight, removes the surge constraint, and frees inverter headroom for other loads.

Other load management measures ranked by typical impact: switch all lighting to LED (saves 0.5-1 kWh/day), replace old chest freezers (1-2 kWh/day savings), schedule washing machines and pumping to midday solar surplus, and eliminate phantom loads via switched extension strips (0.3-0.8 kWh/day).

A practical nightly energy budget for an 18 kWh bank at 90 percent DoD is about 16 kWh usable: one inverter AC (4 kWh), fridge-freezer (1.2 kWh), fans and LED lighting (1 kWh), router/CCTV/standby (0.7 kWh) totals about 7 kWh, leaving margin for a second AC or cloudy-day carryover.
