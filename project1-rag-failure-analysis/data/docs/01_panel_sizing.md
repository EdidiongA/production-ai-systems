# Solar Panel Array Sizing and Orientation

A residential solar array is sized from daily energy consumption, not peak load. To size an array, first compute daily consumption in watt-hours (Wh), then divide by the site's peak sun hours (PSH). Lagos, Nigeria averages 4.5 to 5.0 peak sun hours per day across the year, with the July-September rainy season dropping effective PSH to around 3.5.

Formula: Array wattage = Daily consumption (Wh) / (PSH x system efficiency). A realistic end-to-end system efficiency for an MPPT-based hybrid system is 0.75 to 0.80 after accounting for temperature derating, wiring losses, inverter conversion loss, and battery round-trip loss.

Example: a household consuming 12,000 Wh/day in Lagos needs 12,000 / (4.5 x 0.78) = approximately 3,420 W of panels. Nine 620 W panels (5,580 W) provide roughly 60 percent headroom, which covers rainy-season deficits and battery recharge after grid outages.

Panel temperature derating matters in tropical climates: crystalline panels lose about 0.35 percent of output per degree Celsius above 25 C cell temperature. Cell temperatures of 55-65 C are common on Lagos rooftops, implying a 10-14 percent thermal derate at midday.

Orientation: in the northern hemisphere tropics, a tilt angle equal to latitude (6.5 degrees for Lagos) facing south is optimal, but tilts below 10 degrees accumulate dust and reduce self-cleaning by rain. A practical compromise is 10-15 degrees tilt, which sacrifices under 2 percent annual yield.
